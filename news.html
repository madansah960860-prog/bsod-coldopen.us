How we report

Everything in this section is reported by a named person who did the work. We do not aggregate, we do not rewrite other outlets with a fresh headline, and we do not publish a story built entirely on one anonymous source with no documentation behind it.

Sourcing

Where a story rests on figures, we say where the figures came from and how we handled them. The subtitle measurements, for example, were taken from clean installations on default settings at 1080p, photographed at a fixed distance, and measured in pixels by two people independently. When our two measurements disagreed by more than five per cent we threw the reading out and did it again. That is a tedious method and it is the reason the piece took five weeks.

We grant anonymity when someone would lose their job for speaking, and not otherwise. When we do, the article says what category of person the source is and why they are in a position to know. A quote with no attributable context is not evidence, it is decoration.

Corrections

If we get something wrong we correct it at the top of the article, not in a footnote, and we date the correction. The original wording stays visible with a line through it where the change is material. We have issued four corrections since launching, and all four are still listed on the about page, because a corrections log you can quietly empty is not a corrections log.

What we do not cover

We do not run leaks, unannounced hardware rumours, or stories whose only public interest is that someone wanted them kept quiet for commercial reasons. We also do not cover individual people who are not public figures. The test we apply is simple: does knowing this help someone decide what to buy, what to play, or how the industry that makes it actually works. If the honest answer is no, it does not run.
