How the archive works

Nothing published here is ever deleted to tidy up. An article that turned out to be wrong is corrected in place and labelled; an article that has been overtaken by events gets a dated update at the top pointing at the newer piece. Neither is removed. A publication that quietly unpublishes its mistakes is asking you to trust a record it has edited.

Updates versus corrections

These are different things and we keep them separate. A correction means we published something factually wrong: a number, a name, a date, a claim about who did what. It goes at the top, it says what was wrong, and the original wording stays visible with a line through it. An update means the world moved: a company responded, a patch shipped, a decision was reversed. Updates are dated and appended, and they never overwrite what was accurate at the time of writing.

Follow-ups

Roughly a third of what we publish gets a follow-up within a year, and we decide which by a rule rather than by what performed well. If a story identified a specific, checkable thing that somebody said they would fix, we go back and check. The four-day week piece will be revisited in 2027 with a third year of data. The preservation story will be revisited whenever one of the nine games either returns or is confirmed gone for good.

Why the archive is small

We launched in 2021 and we publish perhaps four news stories a month. That is a deliberately low number for a site of this kind, and it is the direct consequence of only running work we did ourselves. There is no wire copy here, no reworded press releases, and no round-ups of other outlets. Two pages of archive is an honest representation of five years of that approach, and we would rather show you that than pad it out.
