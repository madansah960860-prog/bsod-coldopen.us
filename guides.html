What makes a guide worth writing

A guide earns its place here if it survives one test: can a reader do the thing in a single sitting, and know whether it worked. That rules out most of what gets published as a guide elsewhere. Lists of settings with no indication of which ones matter, walkthroughs that assume you have twelve hours spare, and anything whose real conclusion is that you should spend money are all things we do not publish.

Ordered by effect, not by menu

Every guide here is ordered by how much difference each step makes, largest first, and each step says roughly what you should expect to gain. This is the opposite of how most settings guides are written, which is in the order the options happen to appear in the menu. If the first two steps get you eighty per cent of the benefit, we say so, and we say it at the top rather than burying it.

Difficulty means something specific

Beginner means no prior knowledge is assumed and nothing described can damage hardware or lose data. Intermediate assumes you are comfortable in a settings menu and can read a simple graph. Advanced means the guide is long, the steps are specific, and doing half of it will probably be worse than doing none of it. The three-bar indicator on each card carries a text label as well, because a bar chart alone is not an accessible way to communicate a level.

Reading time and updates

Reading times are measured, not estimated from a word count: someone here reads the finished guide aloud at a normal pace and we round up. Each card shows when the guide was last checked, and we re-check every guide at least once a year, because settings menus move and advice that was right in 2024 is often quietly wrong now.

No affiliate links

There are no affiliate links in any guide on this site. Where a guide mentions a product category we do not link to a shop, because the moment a publication earns a percentage of what it recommends, its recommendations stop being worth reading. Advertising on this site is sold and served separately from editorial and never appears inside the body of a guide as if it were a step.
