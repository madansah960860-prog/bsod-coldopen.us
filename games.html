How this library works

A review only enters the library once someone here has finished the game, or has played far enough past the point of diminishing returns that finishing would not change the argument. For a forty-hour strategy game that means forty hours. For a four-hour puzzle game it means four hours and then a second run.

What the number means

Scores are out of 100 and come from six weighted criteria, not from a gut feeling rounded to the nearest five. The weightings differ by genre, because framerate stability matters more in a twitch platformer than in a turn-based strategy game, and the rubric says so out loud on every review page. A 74 is not a failure. It is a game with real strengths and at least one structural problem we could not talk ourselves out of.

We do not adjust a score after publication unless the game itself changes. When it does, as with Ashfall Parade, we re-review it, date the revision, and leave the original text visible underneath. Quietly editing a score is the fastest way for a publication to become worthless.

Filters and platforms

The platform tags describe where we played the game, not everywhere it is sold. If a review says Console, a member of staff played it on a console from beginning to end. Where a game runs on more than one family of hardware and the experience differs materially, that difference is in the body of the review rather than hidden in a tag.

Filtering updates the address bar, so any view you build here can be bookmarked or sent to someone. With JavaScript switched off the filters are hidden and the full library is listed instead, which is the correct fallback: the content was never behind the control.

What is not here

No preview coverage, no early-access impressions presented as verdicts, and nothing written from a press kit. We also do not review games we cannot buy at retail price with our own money, which is slower and occasionally means we miss a launch window. That trade is the whole point of the place.
